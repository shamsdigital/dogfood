**H1:** Adaptive Reuse Construction in Nashville and Middle Tennessee

*Last updated: July 30, 2026*

Adaptive reuse means taking a building designed for one purpose and making it work for another. Stellan Eoin Builders is a licensed Tennessee general contractor (BC 73150) managing adaptive reuse projects across Nashville and Middle Tennessee, and our first job on these projects usually happens before you own the building: figuring out what the change of use will actually trigger, and whether the numbers still work once it does.

## What decides whether an adaptive reuse project pencils?

Usually not the construction. The occupancy change is what drives the code path. When a building's use changes, egress requirements shift, fire protection requirements shift, accessibility obligations shift, and structural and energy code questions open that the original building never had to answer. A warehouse becoming an event space, an office becoming a restaurant, a church becoming apartments: each of those conversions carries a different set of triggers, and the cost of satisfying them is often the difference between a project that pencils and one that does not.

That is why we evaluate the occupancy change before a purchase whenever we can. The construction estimate on the drawings is only part of the number. The rest is what the jurisdiction will require once the use changes, and that is knowable in advance if someone goes looking for it.

## How do you evaluate a building before we buy it?

We look at the building the way the code official will. What is the current occupancy classification, and what does the intended use change it to? What does that change require for egress and exit capacity? Does the fire protection system exist, and if not, what does adding one do to the budget and the structure? What does accessibility compliance require in a building that predates it? Is the structure carrying what the new use will ask of it?

We have worked with permitting departments in more than twenty Tennessee jurisdictions, and how they interpret a change of use varies more than most buyers expect. Knowing the local answer before you close is considerably cheaper than discovering it after.

This evaluation runs through RightStart, our preconstruction process. Feasibility, code path, scope, budget, and schedule get settled in writing before mobilization, and on a reuse project the feasibility step is the one that earns its keep.

## Who manages the construction itself?

We do, as a pure general contracting firm. We do not self-perform. Every trade on the project is a licensed partner we selected, contracted, and manage, and we answer for all of it. Our founder built hands-on for years before running projects, starting in 1993, and reuse work is where that shows most. Existing buildings do not match their drawings. Someone standing in the space has to recognize what the structure is actually doing, and that judgment comes from having done the work, not just scheduled it.

Because we also manage custom home projects, our commercial finish work holds to a residential standard of detail. On reuse projects where the building's character is the reason you bought it, that standard matters.

## Questions we hear most

**What is the biggest risk in an adaptive reuse project?**
Buying the building before understanding what the change of use triggers. Egress, fire protection, accessibility, structural, and energy code requirements all shift when occupancy changes, and those costs arrive whether or not they were in the budget. We evaluate them before purchase so the decision is made with the whole number in view.

**Can you tell us whether a building will work before we purchase it?**
We can evaluate it, yes. We assess the current conditions, the occupancy change, and the likely code path, and we give you a straight read on what the conversion will require. Sometimes the honest answer is that the building is not a good fit for the use, and hearing that before closing is the whole point.

**Do you handle projects that mix reuse with new construction?**
Yes. Many conversions pair an existing structure with an addition, and the two halves carry different code obligations. We manage both under one accountable party.

**Is adaptive reuse more expensive than building new?**
It depends on the building and the use. Reuse can save structure, location, and character that new construction cannot buy, and it can also uncover conditions that no drawing predicted. The honest comparison comes from the feasibility work, not from a rule of thumb.

**Are you licensed for this work?**
Yes. Tennessee Contractor License BC 73150, the state's broadest building classification, and Metro Nashville and Davidson County Master Contractor License MCN151894. Both are publicly verifiable.
